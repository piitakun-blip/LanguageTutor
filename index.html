Accusative-Only:

Dative-Only:

aus, bei, mit, nach, seit, von, zu

💡 Easy German Gender Shortcut:

In German, all nouns have genders (masculine 🔵, feminine 🔴, or neuter 🟢). When nouns become plural, they all use **the same articles and pronouns as feminine nouns** in the Nominative and Accusative (e.g., *die* Kinder), but require specific plural-ending changes in the Dative (*den* Kindern).

Adjective Declension

German adjectives before nouns change their endings according to case, gender/number, and the determiner before them. Adjectives after sein, werden, or bleiben normally stay uninflected.

Weak endings
after der-words

The determiner already shows case/gender clearly, so the adjective is mostly -e or -en.

Mixed endings
after ein-words

When the determiner lacks a visible case/gender ending, the adjective supplies more information.

Strong endings
with no article

The adjective carries most of the visible case/gender information itself.

Before a noun vs after sein
Attributive → ending
der gute Mann

gut is directly before the noun, so it declines.

Predicative → no ending
Der Mann ist gut.

After sein, the adjective normally stays in its base form.
